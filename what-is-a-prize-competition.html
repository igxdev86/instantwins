What is a prize competition?

“Prize competition” is a legal term in Great Britain, not a marketing one. Whether a scheme is a lawful prize competition, an illegal lottery, or gambling that needs a licence turns on a handful of tests in the Gambling Act 2005. This page sets them out without the jargon.

Three things the Act regulates

The Act (section 3) defines gambling as gaming, betting, or participating in a lottery. A scheme can be caught by any one of the three, so it has to clear all of them. Section 339 then says a prize competition is not gambling unless it is one of those three things.

The lottery test

A simple lottery (section 14) has three ingredients: people are required to pay to enter, prizes are allocated to some of them, and the allocation is wholly by chance. Knock out any one ingredient and it is not a lottery. The two lawful ways to do that are a genuine skill element that stops a significant proportion of entrants winning, or a free entry route so that nobody is required to pay.

The free entry route

Schedule 2 of the Act says a scheme does not “require payment” if everyone can enter either by paying or by ordinary post, the free route is publicised so everyone is likely to know about it, and the prize allocation does not differentiate between paid and free entries. That is the model used by most UK competition sites, and by this one. Our version is set out on the free entry route page.

The gaming test

Gaming is playing a game of chance for a prize, and the definition includes a game that is merely presented as involving chance. This is the test casino-style “instant games” on competition sites tend to fail. A free entry route does nothing for gaming, because the Act says gaming is gaming whether or not the player risks losing anything. It is why our instant wins show you a ticket rather than spin a reel.

What “no licence needed” really means

It means the operator believes the scheme clears all three tests. It is a conclusion, not a protection. A scheme that gets one test wrong is unlicensed gambling, and advertising it is an offence too. Treat the phrase as a claim to be checked, not a badge.

Questions people ask

?
Does a prize competition need a Gambling Commission licence?
Not if it is a lawful free draw or genuine skill competition, because those are not gambling. The Commission does not approve or license them; it does act against schemes that turn out to be lotteries.
?
Is there a minimum age?
The law sets none for lawful prize competitions. The DCMS voluntary code and responsible operators require entrants to be 18 or over, and we verify age before any withdrawal.
?
Does this apply in Northern Ireland?
Northern Ireland has its own legislation with a similar free-entry rule for lotteries. This site is open to residents of Great Britain only.
