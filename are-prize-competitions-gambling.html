Are prize competitions gambling?

Some are, some are not, and the ones that say “definitely not” in their footer are not always right. The law decides it scheme by scheme, and the deciding factors are specific enough that you can apply them yourself.

The honest answer

A prize competition is not gambling if it is not gaming, not betting and not a lottery. Each is a separate test. A site can have a textbook free entry route (which deals with the lottery test) and still be running gaming, because the free entry route only operates for the purposes of the lottery provisions.

Where instant games go wrong

Spinning reels, wheels, dice, falling balls, scratch panels. If the player is shown something that looks like chance deciding the outcome, the game is “presented as involving an element of chance” and falls within the definition of a game of chance, even if the prize was fixed in advance. Unlicensed remote gaming is an offence. This is the main reason the games on this site were rebuilt as plain ticket reveals.

Where draws go wrong

A draw with a free entry route is lawful if the route is genuine, publicised and non-differentiating. It goes wrong when postal entrants cannot reach the draws paid entrants can, when they are given different prizes, or when paying buys a bigger prize than a free entry can win.

Where skill competitions go wrong

A skill question that any adult could answer, or that a search engine answers in a second, does not stop a significant proportion of entrants winning, so the competition is treated as a lottery. “What colour is a red bus?” is the classic example.

So what are we?

Draws with a free postal route into the same draw with the same odds, and instant wins that show a pre-allocated prize without any game to play. We believe that is a lawful prize competition. We also say, on every page, that it is subject to written solicitor sign-off, because “we believe” is the right level of confidence for an untested area of law.

Questions people ask

?
Does a free entry route make a site legal?
It deals with one of three tests. A site with casino-style games still has to clear the gaming test, and the free route does not help there.
?
Why do sites say “no licence required”?
Because a lawful prize competition does not need one. The claim is only as good as the analysis behind it.
?
Who enforces this?
The Gambling Commission for the Gambling Act, the CMA and Trading Standards for consumer law, and the ASA for advertising.
